\section{S1. Components of the flow rule}

\subsection{Kinematics and composition}
For obstacle populations $j$ with areal densities $\lambda_j^{-2}$, the combined spacing is $\lambda^{-2}=\sum_j\lambda_j^{-2}$. The number of type-$j$ encounters per unit glide distance is $n_j=\lambda_j^{-2}\lambda$. The waiting time per unit distance is $\sum_jn_j/\nu_j$, which gives Eq.~(1) of the main text. The strength of type $j$ in the mixture uses the combined spacing, $\hat\tau_j=\alpha_j\mu b/\lambda$.

\subsection{Travel}
\begin{equation}
v_g=\frac{\ts b}{B}\exp\left\{-\frac{\Delta H_0}{2\kT}\max\left[\left(1-\left(\frac{\ts}{\tau_P}\right)^p\right)^q-\frac{T}{T_0},0\right]\right\}.
\end{equation}
For Zr, $B$ interpolates smoothly between the kink drag $B_k$ and the free-flight drag $B_0$ as the barrier vanishes, following the original prismatic law.

\subsection{Bypass routes}
Thermal cutting or detachment:
\begin{equation}
\nu_{\rm th}=\nu_0\exp\left\{-\frac{F}{\kT}\left[1-\left(\frac{\ts}{\hat\tau}\right)^p\right]^q\right\}.
\end{equation}
Glide-assisted (local) climb:
\begin{equation}
\nu_{\rm cl}=\frac{v_c}{h_{\rm eff}},\qquad
v_c=\frac{2\pi D}{b\ln(R/r_c)}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\ts\Omega}{\kT}\right)-1\right],\qquad
h_{\rm eff}=h\max\left(1-\frac{\ts}{\hat\tau},10^{-3}\right).
\end{equation}
The height reduction describes a line pushed against the particle, as in local-climb models~\cite{S_BrownHam}. It is what produces threshold-type creep.

\subsection{State}
Forest density follows Kocks--Mecking kinetics with boundary storage and climb-controlled recovery, Eq.~(3) of the main text. Here $k_2=k_{20}\exp(q_2/\kT)$ and $D=D_0\exp(-Q_D/\kT)$. The steady state is the unique positive root of
\begin{equation}
k_1(x+\Lambda_b)-k_2x^2-\frac{K_{cl}D}{\gd}x^4=0,\qquad x=\sqrt\rho .
\end{equation}
The left-hand side is concave, so the root is unique. When the last term dominates, $\tau_a\propto(\gd/D)^{1/3}$, the natural creep law. Solute aging follows Eq.~(4) of the main text and multiplies the Taylor strength by $\sqrt{C/C_0}$. Diffusional flow is
\begin{equation}
\gd_{\rm diff}=42\,\frac{\Omega\tau}{\kT d^2}\left(D_v+\frac{\pi\delta}{d}D_b\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Uniqueness}
With the state fixed by $(\gd,T,\gamma)$, every term in the bracket of Eq.~(1) of the main text is non-increasing in $\ts$ and at least one is strictly decreasing. The map $\ts\mapsto\gd$ is therefore strictly increasing, and for any imposed $\gd$ in its range there is exactly one $\ts$. Each superposed group $\tau_g$ is solved in the same way from its own waiting kinetics. The stress $\tau(\gd)$ may still be non-monotonic, where DSA gives $m<0$, but it is always single-valued.

\section{S2. Derivation of the rate-sensitivity decomposition}
Let $\tau=\tau^\star_{\rm int}+R$, where $\gd=G_{\rm int}(\tau^\star_{\rm int};\bm s)$ is the intrinsic mobility process (travel plus intragranular obstacles in series) and $R$ collects every other contribution: $\tau_a(\bm s)$, superposed groups, and sources. Then
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\tau}{d\ln\gd}=\frac{1}{A}+B,\qquad A\equiv\frac{\partial\ln G_{\rm int}}{\partial\tau^\star_{\rm int}},\qquad B\equiv\frac{dR}{d\ln\gd},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
m=\frac{1}{\tau}\frac{d\tau}{d\ln\gd}=\frac{1+AB}{\tau A},\qquad V^\star=\frac{\kT}{m\tau}=\frac{\kT A}{1+\Lambda}.
\end{equation}
With rule S, $A=\sum_iw_iA_i$ with $w_i=\gd/\gd_i$. With rule P, $A_j=\sum_k(\nu_{jk}/\nu_j)A_{jk}$. The individual sensitivities are:
\begin{align}
A_{\rm kp}&=\frac1\ts+\frac{\Delta H_0pq}{2\kT\tau_P}x^{p-1}(1-x^p)^{q-1},& x&=\frac{\ts}{\tau_P},\\
A_{\rm th}&=\frac{Fpq}{\kT\hat\tau}y^{p-1}(1-y^p)^{q-1},& y&=\frac{\ts}{\hat\tau},\\
A_{\rm cl}&=\frac{\Omega}{\kT}\frac{e^u}{e^u-1}+\frac{1}{\hat\tau-\ts},& u&=\frac{\ts\Omega}{\kT}.
\end{align}
For solute aging acting on a strength $\hat\tau\to\hat\tau s$ with $s=\sqrt{C/C_0}$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\ln(C/C_0)}{d\ln\gd}=-\frac{\sum_iC_i\alpha_ix_ie^{-x_i}}{C/C_0}<0 \qquad (t_a\propto\gd^{-1}),
\end{equation}
so aging gives $B<0$, and $m<0\iff\Lambda<-1$. In the code, $A$ is evaluated by differentiating $\ln G_{\rm int}$ at the stress the intrinsic process requires on its own, and $d\tau/d\ln\gd$ by differentiating the full solution. The identity $1/m=\tau A/(1+\Lambda)$ agrees with finite differences to better than $10^{-5}$ for all three materials.

\section{S3. Material inputs and calibration}
Conventions: $\gd=\dot\epsilon/M_s$ and $\sigma=\tau/M_s$, with $M_s=0.333$ (W, Eurofer) and $0.45$ (Zr). The Zr and Eurofer axial flow stresses are compared as $\sigma/3$. The global $m$ and $V^\star$ are obtained by least-squares fits over the experimental rate triplets.

\textbf{Calibration sequence.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Kink-pair parameters from the low-temperature, high-rate data.
  \item Athermal and obstacle parameters from the plateau.
  \item Aging parameters from the position of the hump and its shift with rate.
  \item Climb and recovery parameters from the high-temperature, low-rate data.
\end{enumerate}
For W the mobility and Kocks--Mecking parameters are those of the original W model, which the present rule reproduces exactly. For Zr the parameters were fitted to the Derep CRSS at two rates, with a weak weight on $m$. For Eurofer-97 all parameters were fitted jointly to the Vanaja flow stresses (three rates, 300--873~K) and the minimum creep rates of Refs.~\cite{S_Fern,S_Yu}. The objective was the sum of squared relative stress errors plus $0.01\times$ the sum of squared $\log_{10}$ rate errors, minimized by bounded Powell iterations. The activation energy of precipitate climb was tied to that of forest recovery. Result: mean flow-stress error 4.1\%; creep rms error 0.55 decades, with 52 of 54 rates within one decade. The diffusional-creep inputs were not fitted: PAG size $d=21~\mu$m, $D_v$ equal to the recovery diffusivity, and $D_b=2\times10^{-4}\exp(-1.8~\mathrm{eV}/\kT)$~m$^2$/s with $\delta=2b$.
